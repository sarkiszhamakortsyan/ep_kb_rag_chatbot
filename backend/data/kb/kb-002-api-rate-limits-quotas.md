# API Rate Limits & Quotas Reference

The OmniCorp REST API (`https://api.omnicorp.io/v3`) protects platform stability with per-workspace rate limits and daily quotas. This reference describes the limits for each plan, how clients should react when limits are reached, and how customers can request higher limits.

## Limits per plan

Rate limits are applied **per workspace**, not per API token. All tokens of a workspace share the same budget.

| Plan | Requests per minute | Daily quota | Burst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | 60 | 10,000 | up to 120 requests for 10 seconds |
| Business | 600 | 250,000 | up to 1,200 requests for 10 seconds |
| Enterprise | 3,000 | 2,000,000 by default (custom quotas available) | up to 6,000 requests for 10 seconds |

The daily quota resets at **00:00 UTC**.

### How the burst works

Limits use a token-bucket algorithm. A workspace can briefly exceed its per-minute limit by up to **2×** for a maximum of **10 seconds**, after which requests are throttled back to the sustained rate. Bursts are intended for short spikes such as a page load, not for bulk jobs.

## Separate limit buckets

Some traffic has its own bucket and does **not** count towards the general per-minute limit:

- **SCIM provisioning**: 300 requests per minute per workspace (Enterprise only; see *SSO & User Provisioning Guide*, kb-001).
- **Bulk export jobs**: at most **5 concurrent export jobs** per workspace; additional jobs are queued. Exports are described in *Data Retention, Backup & GDPR Policy* (kb-003).
- **Outbound webhook deliveries** from OmniCorp to the customer are not counted at all. However, API calls that the customer's webhook receiver makes back to OmniCorp **do** count (see kb-004).

Traffic generated by the built-in **Salesforce connector** counts against the general per-minute limit and the daily quota.

## Rate limit headers

Every API response includes:

| Header | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `X-RateLimit-Limit` | Sustained requests per minute for the workspace |
| `X-RateLimit-Remaining` | Requests left in the current window |
| `X-RateLimit-Reset` | Unix time when the window resets |
| `X-Quota-Remaining` | Requests left in today's daily quota |
| `Retry-After` | Only on 429 responses: seconds to wait before retrying |

## Errors when limits are reached

- **HTTP 429 `RATE_LIMITED`**: the per-minute limit (including burst) was exceeded. The request was not processed and can be retried safely after the `Retry-After` interval.
- **HTTP 403 `QUOTA_EXCEEDED`**: the daily quota is used up. Further requests fail until 00:00 UTC unless a quota increase is granted.

### Recommended client behaviour

1. Always honour `Retry-After` when present.
2. Otherwise use **exponential backoff with jitter**, starting at **1 second** and doubling up to a maximum of **60 seconds**.
3. Stop retrying after 6 attempts and surface the error.
4. Monitor `X-RateLimit-Remaining` and slow down proactively when it drops below 10% of the limit.
5. Prefer bulk endpoints (`/v3/records:batch`, up to 100 records per call) over single-record calls.

Clients that keep sending requests while throttled for more than 15 minutes may be temporarily blocked for 1 hour. Blocks are logged in **Admin Console → Audit Log**.

## Raising limits

- **Starter and Business**: limits cannot be raised individually. The customer can upgrade the plan, or Business customers can buy the **API Boost** add-on, which raises the per-minute limit and daily quota by **50%**.
- **Enterprise**: permanent increases are requested by the CSM through the **Support Portal → Limit Increase** form, with the expected traffic pattern and business justification. Engineering review takes up to **5 business days**.
- **Temporary increases** (Enterprise only) of up to 5× the normal limit for up to **72 hours** can be granted for data migrations or go-live events. They must be requested at least **5 business days in advance**. Such requests are handled as **P4** tickets (see *Support Tiers, SLAs & Escalation Process*, kb-005).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do failed requests count against the limit?** Yes. Every request that reaches the API, including 4xx errors, consumes rate limit and quota. 429 responses themselves do not consume the daily quota.

**Are limits different in the EU, US and APAC regions?** No. The limits are identical in every data region.

**Can a customer see its API usage history?** Yes. **Admin Console → API → Usage** shows per-day request counts for the last 90 days.
